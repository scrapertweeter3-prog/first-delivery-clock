Twenty-four hours to a working board

A development subscription at $5,995 a month promises onboarding in one day: request cards land on a Trello board, Requests to Active to Approved, within 24 hours, and the first delivery rides a 72 hour turnaround. Three days from payment to merged work is about $856 of runway, 14.29 percent of the monthly price at $285.48 per business day. The agency version of the same first week opens with a statement of work, a procurement review, and a kickoff call, four weeks before the first demo, one full $12,000 retainer month spent before anyone sees running code. The hire version needs six weeks of searching, four weeks of notice at the old job, and three weeks of ramp, 13 weeks, a whole quarter before full speed. That quarter costs 3 x $18,290 = $54,870 loaded, against $17,985 for the subscription's first quarter, 3.05 times the money to reach the same output.

Latency bills separately from fees. A feature that carries $300 of margin per business day withholds $6,000 during the agency's four week intake and $19,500 during a hire's empty quarter, 3.25 subscription months of value parked in a procurement queue. The subscription's intake is a card moved the morning it is written, and the 72 hour promise answers the question while the agency is still collecting security questionnaires.

Intake speed also decides which questions get asked at all. Small work, a scraper, a webhook, an ETL job, dies in procurement when the intake costs more than the answer, and survives as a line on the board when a card can go active tomorrow and merge by Thursday. First delivery at day three runs $856.43 of runway against $12,000 to an agency's first demo, 14 times the spend for one identical question: when does working software arrive.

Before signing anything, put one number on every option: days from payment to first merged work. One answer is 3, one is 20, one is 65. Multiply each by what a day of the feature is worth, and the sticker prices stop being the expensive part of the deal.
